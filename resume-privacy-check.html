Resume privacy check

Ten quick questions about where your CV and job-search data live, then a score and a short list of fixes, biggest first. No sign-up. Your answers stay in this page and disappear when you close it.

HireWire · last reviewed 2026-10-15

Question 1 of 10 Is your CV in a job board’s searchable CV database, visible to recruiters?

Check the visibility setting on every job board where you uploaded a CV. Make it private, or hide your contact details, when you’re not actively looking, and delete old CVs you no longer use.

Question 2 of 10 Can a stranger open a PDF of your CV through a public link (personal website, a cloud folder shared with “anyone with the link”, a public code repository)?

Take your phone number and street address out of any public version, or replace the public PDF with a short page that only shows an email address. Review your cloud sharing links and switch old ones to “restricted”. Search engines can index public PDFs.

Question 3 of 10 Which personal details are on your CV?

An employer doesn’t need your street address or date of birth to invite you to an interview. A photo and date of birth are still common in Germany, but no law requires them. Name, email, phone and city are enough; everything else can come later in the process.

Question 4 of 10 Where do you track your applications?

Close the accounts you no longer use, after exporting anything you want to keep. In the EU, GDPR Article 17 gives you the right to have your personal data erased.

Question 5 of 10 Do you use browser extensions on job sites (autofill, “save this job”, AI helpers)?

Open your browser’s extensions page, remove the ones you don’t use, and limit the rest to “on click” or to specific sites. An extension with access to all websites can read every page you open, not only job ads.

Question 6 of 10 Have you pasted your CV into an AI chatbot or an AI CV-review tool?

Many AI services let you choose whether your conversations are used to improve their models: look for a data or privacy setting before you paste a CV. Remove your phone number and address first, since the AI doesn’t need them, and delete old chats that contain your CV.

Question 7 of 10 How many CV builders, CV scanners or career sites have an account with your CV in it?

Make a list: search your inbox for “welcome” and “verify your email”. Delete the accounts you don’t use. Under GDPR Article 15 you can ask any service which data it holds about you.

Question 8 of 10 Do recruitment agencies send your CV to employers?

Ask each agency, in writing, to send your CV only after you’ve agreed to each specific employer. That keeps track of where your CV is, and avoids the same CV reaching one employer twice.

Question 9 of 10 While you’re still employed, is your job search visible to everyone online (for example a public “open to work” badge)?

If your current employer shouldn’t know, limit “open to work” signals to recruiters only. Platform settings reduce who sees them, but no setting can guarantee that recruiters at your own company never do.

Question 10 of 10 Which email address do you apply from?

Don’t apply from your work address: your employer can usually access that mailbox, and you lose it the day you leave. A separate address for the search keeps recruiter mail apart from your main inbox and is easy to close afterwards.

Why these ten questions

A CV is one of the most detailed documents you hand out: name, contact details, employers, often a photo and date of birth. During a search it gets copied into job boards, trackers, CV scanners, recruiter databases, AI tools and browser extensions. None of that is wrong in itself. The questions cover the places where copies pile up and are easy to forget:

  • Searchable CV databases and public PDFs: anyone who can search or follow the link can read it, and public files can end up in search engines.
  • What’s on the CV: the less personal detail it carries, the less any leak or over-share matters.
  • Trackers, CV builders and scanners with accounts: each one is another copy to keep track of and, eventually, delete.
  • Browser extensions: an extension that can read every website sees more than the job ads it was installed for.
  • AI tools: some services may use conversations to improve their models unless you change a setting.
  • Agencies, visibility and email: who forwards your CV, who can see that you’re looking, and which mailbox the replies land in.

Your rights in the EU

Under the GDPR you can ask any company that holds your CV which data it has about you (Article 15, right of access) and have it deleted when there’s no longer a reason to keep it (Article 17, right to erasure). Most services have a delete-account option or a privacy contact for this; a short email naming the article is enough.

FAQ

Are my answers sent anywhere?

No. The score is worked out by JavaScript in this page. Nothing is uploaded, stored or logged, and HireWire’s guide and tool pages load no analytics. Like every HireWire page, it loads its fonts from Google Fonts.

Is a high score bad?

It means your CV is in many places, some of them public. That’s common during an active search and easy to reduce: work through the fixes from the top, and repeat the check after your search ends.

Does the check judge specific job boards or AI tools?

No. Services and their settings change too often for that. The questions ask about what you did and what you can check yourself, such as a visibility setting or an extension’s permissions.

How do I get my CV deleted from a site?

Look for “delete account” in its settings first. If there isn’t one, write to its privacy contact asking for erasure under Article 17 GDPR, and ask under Article 15 which data they hold if you’re unsure what’s there.

Is there a German version?

Yes: Lebenslauf-Datenschutz-Check, with the same questions and scoring.
